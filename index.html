Diplomatic5 Sep evening Kremlin (talks ~3h10; envoys left ~23:00 CEST); White House and Ushakov readouts overnight into 6 Sep

Putin–Witkoff–Kushner Kremlin session ends without an announced deal; both sides call talks useful and defer next steps to coming weeks

U.S. Special Envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met President Vladimir Putin at the Kremlin on Saturday evening, 5 September. Kremlin foreign-policy aide Yuri Ushakov said the talks lasted three hours and 10 minutes, continued over dinner, and were meaningful, constructive, extremely frank and trusting.[1][2] Ushakov said the Americans put forward a number of ideas on possible routes to a settlement and that the discussion also covered potential U.S.–Russian commercial projects; he did not describe the ideas.[2][3] A White House official, speaking on background to multiple outlets, said the two sides discussed substantive plans for next steps, which will be announced in the coming weeks.[4][5] Putin, in remarks carried by Russian state media and quoted by Ukrainska Pravda, said he had ordered a halt to strikes on Kyiv from midnight 5 September after a request arrived through official channels, both intelligence channels and Foreign Ministry channels, added that Ukrainian strikes on Russian territory including Moscow had significantly reduced, and stated explicitly that Moscow never agreed to Ukraine’s public call for a broader three-day ceasefire.[6] Stake: Ushakov and Peskov speak for the Kremlin; the unnamed White House official speaks for the U.S. administration; neither is a joint communiqué.

Judgment: the public product of the Moscow stop is process language plus a capitals-only pause already announced before the meeting, not a ceasefire or a published plan. The coming-weeks formula from the White House, paired with Putin’s restated refusal of a front-wide pause and Ushakov’s use of Russia’s standing root-causes vocabulary, is consistent with a shuttle that is keeping a channel open rather than closing a gap on terms.

Confidence: High that the meeting occurred and lasted about three hours (Kremlin pool photography, Ushakov on the clock, RIA/TASS departure timing, and independent Western corroboration). Moderate on substance: no document was released, and ideas / next steps remain unspecified by both governments.

Capitals pause does not cover the rest of Ukraine: 108 drones overnight, at least eight killed and 69 injured outside Kyiv city

Stake: casualty figures are Ukrainian official and local-administration counts; Russia’s Ministry of Defence had not issued a matching denial or claim set located in this window.

Judgment: the 5–6 September night confirms the previous cycle’s lead — the pause is geographically narrow. Killing continues in Kharkiv, Kherson, Zaporizhzhia and Kyiv Oblast while envoys move between capitals. That pattern is better read as a facilitation measure for the shuttle than as de-escalation.

Confidence: Moderate-to-high on the Air Force launch/intercept counts and on named-oblast deaths where governors and SES align. The nationwide 8/69 roll-up is a compilation that moved during the morning and should be treated as a floor, not a final forensic total.
